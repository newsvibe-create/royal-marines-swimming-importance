Why Swimming Skills Are Important in Marine Training

When most people think about Royal Marines training, they picture running, marching and assault courses. Swimming is sometimes overlooked, yet it is a fundamental part of being a Commando. The Royal Marines are an amphibious force, which means they operate from the sea and are often required to work in and around water. For that reason, confidence and competence in the water are not optional extras; they are essential skills that every recruit must develop.

Swimming matters first and foremost for safety. Marines may need to cross water, operate from boats, or work in wet conditions where the ability to swim could be the difference between safety and danger. Training includes water-based activities that test comfort in the water, the ability to stay afloat, and the capacity to move efficiently while wearing kit. A recruit who is nervous in the water will struggle, not only with the swimming itself but with the confidence needed for the wider training.

Beyond safety, swimming is also a measure of overall fitness and composure. It requires a combination of cardiovascular endurance, strength and technique. Unlike running, where you can slow down and catch your breath, swimming demands continuous effort and controlled breathing. This makes it an excellent test of how a candidate performs under pressure. Instructors observe whether a candidate stays calm, keeps a steady rhythm, and continues even when tired — qualities that matter in every part of Commando training.

Swimming as Part of the Assessment

Swimming is assessed at several stages of the recruitment and training process, including during the Commando Preparation Course. Candidates are expected to be able to swim competently before they attend, and those who have not spent much time in the water should start practising well in advance. The marine swimming requirements are demanding for a reason: they reflect the real demands of the role, where water confidence is a core professional skill.

How to Improve Your Swimming

If swimming is a weakness, the good news is that it can be improved with regular practice. Start by building basic water confidence: practise floating, treading water and swimming steady lengths of the pool. Focus on technique as well as fitness, because efficient swimming uses far less energy than struggling through the water. Breaststroke and front crawl are both useful, and practising in a variety of conditions — including wearing a T-shirt in the pool, if your local pool allows it — can help you get used to the feeling of swimming with extra weight and resistance.

It is also worth practising swimming when you are already tired, for example after a run or a gym session. This mirrors the conditions of training, where you will often be asked to perform in the water after other physical activity. Building this familiarity reduces anxiety and improves performance when it counts.

A Skill That Lasts a Career

Swimming is not just something to get through during selection. It remains a core skill throughout a Marine's career, used in amphibious operations, water-based exercises and adventure training. Investing time in the pool before you apply is therefore an investment in your entire career. The stronger your swimming, the more confident you will be in training, and the better you will cope with everything that follows.
